Giving birth in Poland as a foreigner: the complete guide (2026)

Expecting a baby in Poland as an expat? Good news: Polish maternity care is governed by one of Europe's most detailed patient-rights regulations — the Perinatal Care Standard (Dz.U. 2025 poz. 1525, in force since 7 May 2026). It defines the staff's legal obligations, not just good practice. Here is what you need to know.

Your rights during labour

  • A birth companion at every stage — partner, friend or doula; public hospitals cannot charge for this.
  • 2 hours of uninterrupted skin-to-skin contact right after birth; weighing can wait.
  • Informed consent before every intervention — episiotomy, amniotomy and oxytocin are not routine and require explanation of indications and alternatives.
  • Information about all available pain-relief methods, including epidural anaesthesia.
  • Freedom of movement, choice of birthing positions, and access to water immersion where available.

Insurance and costs (NFZ)

If you are employed in Poland or hold voluntary NFZ insurance, giving birth in a public hospital is free. Crucially, childbirth is classified as emergency care — a hospital must admit a woman in labour regardless of paperwork; formalities can be sorted afterwards. Private hospital births typically cost 8,000–20,000+ PLN.

Choosing a hospital

There is no catchment-area rule: you may choose any public hospital in Poland. Compare caesarean rates, epidural availability and family rooms on gdzierodzic.info (run by the Childbirth with Dignity Foundation). English-speaking staff are common in large cities but never guaranteed on every shift — which brings us to the single most useful document you can prepare.

Practical checklist

  • Prepare your birth plan between weeks 32 and 36 and discuss it with your doctor or midwife.
  • Bring: ID/passport, insurance confirmation, pregnancy card (karta ciąży), blood group result, HBsAg and GBS results.
  • Print two copies of the plan: one for the medical records, one for your hospital bag.
  • After birth: the hospital reports the birth; you collect the birth certificate at the civil registry office (USC) — needed for your embassy paperwork.

Frequently asked questions

Can I give birth in Poland without Polish citizenship or residency?

Yes. Childbirth is emergency care, so hospitals must admit any woman in labour. Costs depend on your insurance: NFZ-insured mothers give birth for free in public hospitals; otherwise you will be billed or can use private care.

Do hospitals in Poland speak English?

In large cities many doctors and midwives speak English, but it is never guaranteed on every shift. A birth plan in Polish removes the risk — staff can read your preferences even when no English speaker is present.

Can my partner attend the birth?

Yes — the Perinatal Care Standard guarantees the right to a companion at every stage of labour, including (hospital permitting) caesarean sections, free of charge in public hospitals.

Is an epidural available in Polish hospitals?

Epidural anaesthesia is available in most larger hospitals and you have the right to information about all pain-relief methods. Availability can depend on the anaesthesiologist on duty, so ask your chosen hospital in advance and state your preference in the birth plan.
